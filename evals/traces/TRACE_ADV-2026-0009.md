## 5. Document shape: a narrative report with an indicator-list SECTION

Read from the PDF directly (`data/advisories/fincen-kleptocracy-2022.pdf`,
pages 8-9, page index 7-8): the document is a 13-page narrative advisory for
pages 1-7, followed by a labelled **"Financial Red Flag Indicators of
Kleptocracy and Foreign Public Corruption"** section — a ten-bullet list —
before the SAR/BSA boilerplate on pages 9-13. Four of the six misses'
citations come straight from that bulleted section:

- PAT002: "Transactions involving long-term government contracts consistently
  awarded ... to the same legal entity or entities that share similar
  beneficial ownership structures." (bullet 1, p.8)
- SAN008: "Transactions involving services provided to state-owned companies
  or public institutions by companies registered in high-risk jurisdictions."
  (bullet 2, p.8)
- TBML001: "Documents corroborating transactions involving government
  contracts (e.g., invoices) that include charges at substantially higher
  prices than market rates ..." (bullet, p.9)
- PAT003 (2nd citation): "Assets held in the name of intermediate legal
  entities whose beneficial owner or owners are tied to a kleptocrat ..."
  (bullet, p.9)

This is a hybrid document: narrative body + a red-flag-list appendix. The
golden labeller applied the **restated** rule from `evals/golden/README.md`
("in an indicator-list document ... each indicator ... may carry a typology
... a single bullet is sufficient evidence") to the appendix, mining four
separate low/medium-confidence governed links out of it.

**Every one of the 10 agent runs does the opposite.** `adopt_rep3`'s notes say
it explicitly: "The ten bulleted financial red flags (pp.8-9) are treated as
indicators of the corruption typology rather than as separate typologies,
since the document is a narrative advisory built around named typology
sections ... rather than a pure indicator-list format." The agent classifies
the WHOLE document by its dominant shape (narrative) and, having made that
call, folds the appendix bullets into the one or two typologies it already
identified from the body — the same judgement the golden labelling rule was
restated on 2026-09-11 to overturn (see `evals/golden/README.md`,
"Labelling conventions"), on a document the agent evidently never re-ran
against the restated rule. This is a *document-shape misclassification*, not
a raw output-length limit: it happens even in runs (main: 19 searches) that
demonstrably had plenty of "search budget" left.


## 6. Retrievability probes (`kc.rank_typologies`, floor 0.25)

For each miss: the golden citation's own quote, plus 2-3 queries phrased from
the doctrine's own likely vocabulary (never mined from the citation text
itself, per the hard rule against circularity — though the golden quote is
also shown, as the task requires, to test the document's own wording).

**PAT010 Hidden Controller**
- Golden quote 1 ("family members and close associates ... retaining control
  of the accounts") -> `[(0.75, TBML009), (0.61, PAT007), (0.27, BA003)]` —
  PAT010 absent.
- Golden quote 2 ("legal entities that are controlled by family members and
  close associates") -> `[(0.69, TBML008)]` — PAT010 absent.
- "family members and close associates retain control of accounts on behalf
  of a corrupt official" (agent's own likely framing) -> **no match above
  floor**.
- "nominee controls an entity on behalf of the true beneficial owner" ->
  `[(0.69, PAT002), (0.53, SAN001), (0.52, PAT010), ...]` — PAT010 appears,
  weakly.
- "hidden controller behind a company" -> `[(1.00, PAT010), (1.00, SAN004),
  (1.00, TBML009), ...]` — PAT010 at ceiling.

**PAT010 is a vocabulary gap, not a missing doctrine entry.** The document's
own phrasing ("family members and close associates retaining control")
scores nothing; the doctrine's indexed vocabulary is keyed to "nominee" and
"hidden controller" almost exclusively. The single run that used that exact
framing (`rep1`: "beneficial ownership hidden controller nominee assets")
found it at 1.00, confirmed it with `get_typology`, and asserted it. Nine
other runs never phrased a query close enough.

**PAT002 Shared Beneficial Owner**
- Golden quote -> `[(1.00, BA001), (0.70, PAT002), (0.69, SAN001), ...]` —
  PAT002 present at rank 2, comfortably above floor.
- "multiple companies share the same beneficial owner" -> `[(1.00, FND003),
  (1.00, PAT002), (1.00, PAT007), ...]` — ceiling.

**PAT002 is retrievable, including from the golden citation itself.** Two of
the ten runs (`adopt_rep1`, `adopt_rep2`) surfaced it at 0.69 via their own
queries ("third party facilitator payments hide beneficial owner", "senior
foreign political figure PEP beneficial owner shell company") — well above
floor — and neither called `get_typology` on it or asserted it. The other
eight runs never issued a query containing "beneficial owner" at all.

**SAN008 High Risk Jurisdictions**
- Golden quote 2 ("companies registered in high-risk jurisdictions") ->
  `[(1.00, SAN008), (0.81, CMI010), (0.81, SAN002), ...]` — a clean hit at
  ceiling, straight from the label's own citation.
- "high risk jurisdiction with limited transparency used to establish shell
  companies" -> `[(1.00, SAN008), (0.75, TBML009), (0.75, PAT007), ...]`.

**SAN008 is trivially retrievable — this is the cleanest miss in the set.**
`rep1` searched "high risk jurisdictions offshore financial centers", got
SAN008 at 1.00, called `get_typology('SAN008')` to confirm it — **and then did
not assert it.** No other run searched anything containing "jurisdiction" at
all. So the miss is D (never searched) in 9 of 10 runs and a bare A
(retrieved, confirmed, dropped anyway) in the tenth. There is no retrieval
excuse available for `rep1`'s drop.

**BA008 Layering**
- Golden quote ("bribes paid through a complex network of shell companies,
  off-book transactions, and off-shore bank accounts") -> `[(1.00, PAT007),
  (0.75, TBML009)]` — **BA008 itself does not clear the floor from its own
  citation.**
- "bribes routed through a complex layered network of shell companies and
  offshore accounts" -> `[(1.00, BA008), (1.00, CM004), (1.00, PAT007), ...]`
  — adding the word "layered" is what surfaces it.

**BA008 is a genuine vocabulary gap on its OWN citation** (the doctrine's
term is "layering"; the document never uses that word for this fact pattern)
**but it showed up as noise in nearly every run anyway** — appearing at
0.21-0.73 attached to eight different, mostly unrelated, queries across all
ten runs ("misappropriation...", "luxury real estate...", "straw purchaser
...", etc.), confirmed via `get_typology` in 3 of 10 runs (main, rep2,
prefix_rep2), and asserted in exactly one (`prefix_rep2`). This is the
clearest example of classification A operating at scale: the tool keeps
surfacing a weak, generic candidate, and in 9 of 10 cases the agent looks at
it (or a search result containing it) and declines.

**TBML001 Over Invoicing**
- Golden quote ("invoices ... substantially higher prices than market
  rates") -> `[(0.37, TBML002U), (0.32, TBML001), (0.32, TBML002), ...]` —
  present, but barely, and behind two siblings.
- "over invoicing trade goods to transfer value" -> `[(1.00, TBML001), (1.00,
  TBML002), (1.00, TBML002U), (1.00, TBML003), ...]` — ceiling, but this
  query uses the doctrine's own term ("over invoicing"), which nothing in the
  advisory's text does; the advisory never says "over-invoicing" or
  "trade-based" anywhere.
- "overpriced contract used to disguise a bribe payment" (closer to how the
  document actually frames it) -> **no TBML* result above 0.26**.

**TBML001 sits right at the edge of retrievability from the document's own
language**, and worse from a bribery-flavoured paraphrase of it. It surfaced
as noise in three runs (main 0.27, rep2 0.34, `prefix_rep3` 0.25 — the last
attached to a query that WAS on-target, "fake invoices to justify payments
false documentation," and still only just cleared the floor). No run ever
called `get_typology('TBML001')`. This is a genuine mechanism gap: the
doctrine's TBML family is written for cross-border trade invoicing, and nothing
in a "government contract invoice" framing reliably reaches it above 0.30-0.37
even when the underlying quote is a near match in meaning.

## 8. Verdict

**This document does not cleanly support the pure "fixed assertion budget"
reading, and in one respect actively argues against it.** If a hard output
quota (~4-5 items, the estate mean) were the binding constraint, a rational
agent that had already retrieved 6 of the 7 golden ids somewhere across its
ten independent attempts (PAT007, PAT010, PAT002, PAT003, SAN008, BA008 all
surfaced above floor in at least one run; only TBML001 stayed marginal) would
be expected to assert closer to that ceiling once it found them. Instead the
asserted count tops out at 2 in every single run — well under any plausible
per-run budget observed elsewhere in the estate. **The constraint on THIS
document is retrieval coverage per run, not exhaustion of an assertion
allowance:** most of the misses were never found because the query that would
have found them was never tried (D: PAT010 in 9/10, PAT002 in 8/10, SAN008 in
9/10), and the ones that WERE found were usually left uncommitted (A: PAT002,
PAT003, SAN008-in-rep1, BA008-in-9/10) rather than traded off against
something else.

**What DOES support the estate-wide finding, on this document specifically:**

1. The pre-search-fix run's own notes are direct first-person evidence of a
   prompt-driven suppression event ("left out per 'fewer, well-cited items'"
   naming BA008 and PAT003 specifically) — proof that the mechanism the
   estate-wide finding describes is real and has fired on this exact
   document.
2. That the same two items are STILL missing in 9 of 10 post-fix runs shows
   the prompt-line removal did not repair the underlying behaviour here —
   consistent with `FULL_BASELINE_2026-09-12.md` naming ADV-2026-0009 as one
   of the five advisories whose recall did not move.
3. A genuinely NEW mechanism, not previously traced (classification E): the
   agent classifies this hybrid document as "narrative" and folds its
   ten-bullet red-flag appendix into the one or two typologies already found
   in the body, rather than mining each bullet per the golden set's OWN
   restated labelling rule for indicator sections. This happens even in the
   19-search main run, so it is not an artefact of running out of search
   budget — it is a document-shape judgement made early and never revisited.

**Cannot distinguish, and the measurement that would separate it:** whether
the "narrative vs. indicator-list" misjudgement (mechanism 3, above) is itself
just the assertion-budget wearing a different hat — i.e., whether a run given
explicit instruction to treat page 8-9 as its own indicator-list section
would then hit a NEW ceiling at 4-5 items (reproducing the estate-wide
pattern one level down) or would actually recover most of the 7 golden ids
(disproving a hard ceiling on this document specifically). That is a live-run
experiment, not something this trace of existing artefacts can settle, and it
was explicitly out of scope here (ANALYSIS ONLY, no extractions).

**Bottom line:** the extreme recall on ADV-2026-0009 is better explained as
*"the agent doesn't reliably search for, or commit to, per-bullet typologies
in a red-flag-list appendix it has already mentally filed under one narrative
topic"* than as *"the agent found plenty and an output quota cut it off."*
Both are failures of the same observable shape (a near-constant low count
against a high golden target) but they point at different week-4 fixes: a
budget fix would relax a stopping rule; this document's fix is a retrieval
and document-shape one — search each indicator bullet on its own terms, and
stop collapsing a mixed-shape document into its dominant section.
