# Trace: ADV-2026-0017 (NCA/NECC Shadow Fleet red alert) — why recall did not move

Working notes. ADV-2026-0017 is the UK red alert "Shadow Fleet Sanctions Evasion
and Avoidance Network" (0774-NECC, 8 pages). Golden holds 8 governed typologies
plus 3 emergent labels. It is the one document of the five studied whose recall
was identical before and after the 2026-09-12 prompt change
(0.25/0.25/0.38 -> 0.25/0.25/0.38). This trace finds why: the document's
dominant failure is not the assertion-budget/document-shape mechanism the
2026-09-12 edit targeted, but a **twin-resolution defect** the prompt's fix
never touched.

## 4. extraction_notes: the agent's own account

Two runs give a direct, first-person explanation for the SAN006 miss, and it
is a **coherent doctrinal argument**, not an oversight:

> "SAN006 (Trade Based Sanctions Evasion, mispricing/misdescription) was
> considered but not linked, as the document does not describe invoice
> mispricing or goods misdescription -- only origin-obscuring via
> mixing/transhipment and trade through front companies, better matched by
> TBML010." — `rep2`

> "TBML010 link is medium confidence as the advisory does not use
> invoice/trade-document mispricing language explicitly (that sits more with
> SAN006), but the underlying pattern of using ordinary commercial oil trade
> to maintain access for a sanctioned party fits TBML010's doctrine. SAN006
> ... and SAN001 ... were considered but not proposed as the specific
> mispricing/misdescription and >=50% control-path elements they require are
> not stated in this document." — `rep3`

Both runs read SAN006's **indicator list** literally ("Large price deviation
from benchmark", "Goods misdescription") and correctly note the document
never describes an over/under-priced invoice. Both conclude TBML010 fits
better because TBML010's indicators ("Sanctioned Party", "Related Party
Trading") match the worked example's actual content (a within-network resale
to reach an end customer). This is a defensible reading of SAN006's
**indicators**, and a wrong reading of SAN006's **doctrine**: SAN006's summary
sentence is broader than its indicator list — "moves value or prohibited
goods across a sanctioned boundary by manipulating the trade transaction
itself" — and the golden labeller cited exactly the within-network-resale
sentence for SAN006, reading the resale itself as the transaction being
manipulated. Neither the agent's system prompt nor the tool surface exposes
this distinction; the agent only ever sees the indicator list via
`get_typology`, not a ruling on which sentence in the summary controls.

Other notes are consistent and add no new mechanism: jurisdiction inference
caveats (DMCC/PTE suffixes), the OCCRP footnote (Kazakhstan/Belarus) correctly
excluded as not asserted in-body, and (in `adopt_rep2`/`adopt_rep3`) explicit,
correct resolution of the PAT008/SAN002 "Shadow Fleet" label collision in
SAN002's favour — "PAT008 (network family) also matched 'Shadow Fleet' ...
but SAN002 (sanctions family) was selected as the closer fit given this is a
sanctions-evasion advisory." No run's notes mention SAN009, PAT009, SAN008 or
PAT007 by name at all — they are absent even as "considered and rejected"
items, which is the signature of classification D, not A, for most of their
misses (see section 6).

## 6. Retrievability probes and per-typology classification

Method: `kc.rank_typologies(query)`, floor 0.25, per the task's required
check. For each miss: the golden citation's own quote, plus doctrine-derived
queries (never mined from the citation).

### SAN006 Trade Based Sanctions Evasion — **0/10, the dominant finding**

- Golden quote ("the refined product is sold to another company within the
  same network...") -> `[(0.68, PAT007), (0.26, SAN002)]` — **SAN006 itself
  does not clear the floor from its own citation.**
- Doctrine phrase ("mispricing an invoice or misdescribing goods to move
  value across a sanctioned boundary") -> `[(1.00, SAN006), (0.52, TBML001),
  ...]` — ceiling.
- Doctrine phrase 2 ("trade transaction manipulated to evade sanctions") ->
  `[(0.90, SAN006), (0.65, CM002), ..., (0.63, TBML010), ...]` — SAN006 beats
  TBML010 when the query uses SAN006's own summary language.

**But this is not what the agent actually searched.** Every one of the ten
runs' actual queries ("shadow fleet oil tankers sanctions evasion",
"sanctions evasion oil trading network blending origin", "Russian oil
sanctions evasion network front companies", etc.) surfaces **both** SAN006
(0.50-0.70) **and** TBML010 (0.77-1.00) together, with TBML010 always scoring
higher. `get_typology('SAN006')` was called and returned a real result in
**8 of 10 runs** (all but `rep1` and `prefix_rep2`, where it appeared in
search but was never confirmed). In every single run, regardless of
confirmation, `propose_link` was never called for SAN006.

**Classification: A, dominant, at scale.** This is a stronger version of the
0009 trace's SAN008 case — there, one run confirmed and dropped a perfect
hit; here, eight of ten runs confirm the correct typology via `get_typology`
and drop it in favour of its higher-scoring twin, every time. See section 8
for the twin-resolution mechanism.

## 8. The twin check

`evals/golden/README.md`'s "twins across families" convention names SAN006
(sanctions) / TBML010 (tbml) as a pair, and rules that a sanctions-framed
advisory should label the sanctions twin — exactly the situation here (an
NCA/OFSI/FCDO red alert). The golden label follows the rule: SAN006, not
TBML010.

**The agent gets this backwards in 8 of 10 runs, and it is not a random
miss.** `agents/extract_advisory.py`'s system prompt already has a rule for
this class of problem — "Two typologies can share a label; choose by family.
When a result says its doctrine is authored as another id, prefer that id."
— and it works: PAT008 and SAN002 **share the literal label "Shadow Fleet"**,
and every one of the 10 runs correctly resolves that pair to SAN002 (the
sanctions-family twin), several runs' notes stating the reasoning explicitly
("PAT008 ... also matched 'Shadow Fleet' ... but SAN002 ... was selected as
the closer fit given this is a sanctions-evasion advisory").

**SAN006 and TBML010 do not share a label** ("Trade Based Sanctions Evasion"
vs. "Sanctions Evasion Through Trade"), so the prompt's same-label heuristic
never fires for this pair. Resolution falls back to raw search score and
indicator-list literalism — and TBML010 outscores SAN006 in every query that
returns both (0.77-1.00 vs. 0.50-0.70; see section 6) — so the agent
consistently, confidently, and articulately (section 4) picks the wrong twin.

**This is not "splits across both."** No run asserts SAN006 and TBML010
together; in the 8/10 runs with the defect, TBML010 is asserted and SAN006 is
not, cleanly. The scorer therefore counts, in each of those 8 runs, one false
negative (SAN006 missed) plus one false positive (TBML010 wrong) for what is,
functionally, a single mapping decision made once per run — the agent applied
its retrieval-and-confirmation process correctly and picked the wrong branch
at the family-selection step the prompt does not cover for this pair. That
inflates the apparent error count relative to what actually went wrong: fix
one selection rule and both the FN and the FP disappear together, without
touching anything else the agent does.

The two runs that avoid the TBML010 mistake (`prefix_rep3`, `adopt_rep3`) do
not recover SAN006 either — `prefix_rep3` substitutes a second wrong id
(SAN001 Ownership Evasion), and `adopt_rep3` simply drops the slot (proposes
SAN002/SAN004/SAN003 and stops, having confirmed SAN006 via `get_typology`
and not proposed it). So avoiding the twin trap does not, on its own, recover
the correct answer here; the underlying "SAN006's indicator list reads as
mispricing-only" misreading (section 4) is doing damage independently of
which sibling wins.

## 9. Verdict: why this document alone did not respond to the prompt change

The 2026-09-12 prompt edit (`agents/extract_advisory.py` docstring, lines
18-31) replaced "prefer fewer, well-cited items" with the document-shape rule,
targeting a documented mechanism on ADV-2026-0016: SAN006 there was retrieved,
confirmed, and dropped **because of the old prompt's budget language**, and
removing that language fixed it (0 of 7 old-prompt runs -> 3 of 3 new-prompt
runs). That is a real, verified fix for the mechanism it targeted.

**ADV-2026-0017's SAN006 miss is retrieved-and-confirmed-and-dropped too, but
for a different, specific reason the prompt change does not touch: it loses a
head-to-head scoring contest against its cross-family twin, TBML010, every
time, because the "choose by family" rule in the prompt only fires when two
candidates share a literal label.** SAN006/TBML010 do not share a label. No
version of the "prefer fewer" vs. "document-shape" prompt wording changes
which of two already-confirmed candidates gets proposed when they don't
collide on the same-label check — that decision was never budget-driven or
shape-driven here; it was always a doctrine/scoring comparison the prompt has
no rule for. This explains, precisely, why removing the budget language moved
recall on other documents and left this one's typology recall flat: **the
